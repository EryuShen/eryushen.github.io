Constructive Dialogue

A 60-minute leadership session that gives a family business a structure for the conversations it keeps avoiding

A third-generation family bottling company is stuck in a leadership transition. The founder still makes every decision, his children want to modernize, and the hard conversations never happen. Our team of six designed a four-module program for its leaders. I designed Module 3, the session where they practice those conversations.

My part
Module 3 end to end: the S.T.A.R. framework, lesson plan, role-play activity, slide deck and Transition Talk Planner worksheet
Learners
The founder, the next generation, the general manager and department heads
Format
60-minute live workshop, in person or on Zoom
Setting
Midwest Bottling, a fictional company from a course case

Where my module fits

The program moves leaders from awareness to action. Module 2 builds the emotional skills. Module 3 is where leaders use them in the conversation they've been putting off.

  1. Module 1The FoundationMapping the company's legacy and shared values
  2. Module 2The EngineManaging emotional triggers in high-stakes talks
  3. My moduleModule 3The BridgeA structured framework for succession conversations
  4. Module 4The BlueprintDrafting the first 90-day transition plan

The team's full program also includes a training needs analysis plan, a Kirkpatrick evaluation plan and transfer-of-learning checkpoints. In that plan, Module 3 is assessed by facilitators observing the role-play with a rubric that scores how participants use S.T.A.R.

The S.T.A.R. framework

Emotional intelligence is abstract. A leader in the middle of a succession conflict needs something to hold onto in the moment. S.T.A.R. turns it into four steps, each with a question to ask yourself, the emotion to bring and one practical move.

SSetting the Stage

"What do I want to achieve from this conversation?"

Calm readiness

Choose the right time and place; clarify the purpose and tone at the start.

TTone of Respect

"How can I show appreciation while being honest?"

Empathy

Begin with gratitude; recognize past contributions before discussing change.

AApproach with Inquiry

"What do I need to understand before offering my view?"

Curiosity

Ask open-ended questions; listen actively; avoid assumptions.

RResolution Orientation

"What is one next step we can both agree on?"

Collaboration

Summarize shared points and agree on next actions before ending.

For this audience, respect comes before inquiry. In a family business, a proposal to modernize is easily heard as criticism of the founder, so the conversation starts by honoring what he built.

The 60-minute session

More than half the session is practice. The input is short, and every part points back to a real conversation each leader will have.

Hook, 10 minThe conversation I'm avoiding
Input, 15 minS.T.A.R., with a defensive vs. constructive example
Practice, 20 minRole-play in triads
Debrief, 10 minWhat felt natural or forced
Commit, 5 minPlanner

Role-play the transition talk

Three roles, rotated so everyone tries each one

  • SpeakerOpens the difficult conversation
  • ListenerPlays the other side
  • ObserverTakes notes and gives feedback

Each triad gets a scenario card from the case

  • AA second-generation leader proposes a new structure to the founder.
  • BThe founder responds to modernization ideas from his children.
  • CThe general manager mediates between siblings about leadership roles.

After a 5-minute dialogue, the observer uses a checklist

  • Did they clarify the purpose?
  • Did they keep a respectful tone?
  • Did they end with next steps?
It opens with their own conversation"The Conversation I'm Avoiding"

Each leader names one conversation about the transition they've avoided and shares in pairs what makes it hard. The goal is to normalize the discomfort and make the session personal from minute one.

It closes with a commitmentThe Transition Talk Planner

Each leader completes one line before leaving: One upcoming conversation I will approach using S.T.A.R. is… and My first step will be…

What I took from it

The company's problem wasn't strategy. Its leaders had no safe, structured way to talk about the change they all knew was coming.

  • Training as behavior changeThe job wasn't to explain emotional intelligence, but to give leaders a structure they could use in their next real conversation.
  • Practice over lectureRole-play and peer feedback gave people a safe place to try new language before using it at work.
  • What I'd add nextSupport after the session, where behavior change really happens: follow-up coaching, conversation practice labs and microlearning job aids.
