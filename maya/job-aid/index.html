Larkmoor Advisory | Using AI in client work
Job aid

Before it goes to the client

Four checks for anything AI helped you make. Most take a few minutes.

Rule of thumbIf you can't open the source yourself, it doesn't go on the slide.

1Should AI do this?

If this is wrong, who catches it, and when?

  • Let AI draft when the stakes are low and you can check the result: outlines, first passes, reformatting.
  • Keep it human when it needs judgment or someone accountable: recommendations, client advice, anything you'll defend in the room.
  • Don't rule AI out by default. An approved tool can save hours on safe tasks.

2Protect the client

Would the client be comfortable seeing where their data went?

  • Use only firm-approved AI tools for client work. Free public tools are off limits.
  • Share only the data the task needs.
  • Take out names, account numbers and anything else that identifies a person or account.
  • Not sure a tool is approved? Ask before you upload, not after.

3Check for the three errors

What would a careful reader catch?

  • Made-up facts. Open every source. Asking the AI "Are you sure?" isn't checking; it can't check itself.
  • Bias. Look for one-sided framing or assumptions about people, markets or options.
  • What's missing. Summaries leave things out. Skim the originals for anything skipped.

4Own it

Could you explain every line if the client asked?

  • Rewrite AI output in your own words.
  • Cite real sources you've opened yourself, in the footnote.
  • Unsure? Ask a colleague before it goes out. Checking the sources first saves their time.

When this happens, do this

SituationWhat to do
AI gives you a number and names a sourceFind and open that source. If it doesn't exist, use a real one and cite it.
You want AI to analyze a client fileUse the approved tool and remove identifying columns first.
AI summarizes several articles for youSkim each original for risks or facts the summary skipped.
